# Major-muscle catalog follow-up — 22 October 2026

A renewed post-push scan against manufacturer catalogs found two lower-body machine movements missing from Atlas: the V-Squat and Vertical Leg Press. A further check of the plate-loaded lineup found eight more distinct machines: independent-arm horizontal and decline chest presses, a Super Squat Press, an Assisted Nordic Hamstring Curl, a D.Y. Row, a Super Incline Press, a machine shrug, and a machine gripper. Later scans added a dedicated tibialis dorsi-flexion machine, Barbell Bulgarian Split Squat, Cable Romanian Deadlift, Smith Machine Romanian Deadlift, and Iso-Lateral Wide Chest Press. A later back-row pass added Meadows Row, a named side-on pronated-sleeve landmine variation. A chest scan against ACE's exercise library found Seated Cable Chest Press absent; its supported seated setup differs from Atlas's standing cable press. A Smith-machine leg scan found Bulgarian Split Squat absent despite existing barbell, dumbbell, and bodyweight versions. Later additions include a popular chest-supported plate-loaded horizontal row, an adjustable plate-loaded chest fly, ACE's straight-bar reverse curl, a plate-loaded front lat pulldown with independent underhand-grip arms and a down/back arc, a Smith Machine Decline Bench Press combining a guided bar with a decline setup, a Smith Machine Close-Grip Bench Press using a shoulder-width triceps-focused grip, and two Hammer Strength plate-loaded machines with independent leg arms: seated knee extension and prone hamstring curl. A Smith squat scan then added the guided Smith Machine Front Squat, absent beside Atlas's barbell front squat and Smith back squat. A selectorized-machine scan then found that the generic Leg Press illustration shows a 45-degree sled and did not cover the common seated weight-stack configuration. A barbell movement scan found the dynamic Push Press absent beside the dumbbell version and strict standing overhead press.

## Why they are distinct

- **V-Squat:** Atlas already contains Hack Squat, Pendulum Squat, Belt Squat, and Smith Machine Squat. The Hammer Strength V-Squat uses a supported curved arc; its manufacturer distinguishes this from the Hack Squat's linear track and the deeper pendulum pattern. The V-Squat is sold as a distinct plate-loaded machine.
- **Vertical Leg Press:** Atlas's general Leg Press uses a seated setup. The vertical model has the athlete lying supine and driving a guided platform directly above the hips, a distinct body position and resistance path. Body-Solid and Panatta each list a dedicated vertical leg-press machine.
- **Horizontal bench press:** Atlas has a selectorized chest press and a plate-loaded iso-lateral incline press, but no plate-loaded horizontal bench press. The Hammer Strength model uses independent arms and a converging/diverging path, rather than the selectorized machine's shared press mechanism.
- **Seated Cable Chest Press:** Atlas has a standing cable press and selectorized seated chest presses, but not the seated, back-supported cable press ACE describes. The cable handles move independently and the supported seated setup differs from both the standing cable press and fixed-arm press machines.
- **Smith Machine Bulgarian Split Squat:** Atlas has Bulgarian split squats with bodyweight, dumbbells, and a free bar, but no guided-bar version. A rear-foot-elevated stance under the Smith bar keeps the same unilateral squat pattern while fixing the bar path; a study directly examined this Smith-machine form separately from the free-weight version.
- **Smith Machine Front Squat:** Atlas has barbell front squats and Smith back squats, but not a front-rack squat using the Smith's guided bar. The bar resting on the front shoulders with elbows raised changes the load position from the existing Smith back squat while retaining the familiar front-squat pattern.
- **Decline chest press:** Atlas has a selectorized decline press, but no independent-arm, plate-loaded decline station. The Hammer Strength machine uses separate arms and a downward-forward path from an upright seated position.
- **Super Squat Press:** Atlas has hack, pendulum, V-Squat, leg press, and belt squat movements. This newer machine adds articulating footplates, a four-bar linkage, and independent work arms for unilateral leg training.
- **Assisted Nordic Hamstring Curl:** Atlas has the unassisted bodyweight Nordic curl and machine knee curls. The counterbalanced machine allows assistance to be adjusted by loading its assistance horns, making the Nordic pattern scalable while retaining its forward-fall eccentric action.
- **D.Y. Row:** Existing rows cover seated, low, high, T-bar, and unilateral patterns. The D.Y. machine adds an underhand grip, overhead pivot, and shoulder-aligned downward/backward arc with chest support.
- **Super Incline Press:** Existing machine shoulder and incline presses cover separate conventional angles. The Super Incline has a steeper upward-forward path between those presses and independent work arms.
- **Machine shrug:** Atlas has cable, barbell, and dumbbell shrugs but no plate-loaded station with fixed side handles and both seated/standing positions.
- **Machine gripper:** Atlas has a spring hand gripper, but not the seated plate-loaded unit with pronated and supinated grip positions. This adds adjustable loaded grip work while retaining the same hand-grip family.
- **Tibia Dorsi-Flexion:** Atlas has a wall-supported tibialis raise but no dedicated machine. The plate-loaded seated device stabilizes the thigh and applies resistance at the forefoot for ankle dorsiflexion, complementing calf-raise work.
- **Barbell Bulgarian Split Squat:** Atlas has bodyweight and dumbbell records but no barbell-loaded version. The bar across the upper back allows a distinct loading progression while preserving the rear-foot-elevated unilateral squat.
- **Cable Romanian Deadlift:** Atlas has barbell and dumbbell RDLs and a cable pull-through. The cable RDL faces the pulley and tracks the handle along the front of the legs, unlike the pull-through's away-facing rope path between the legs.
- **Smith Machine Romanian Deadlift:** Atlas has free-weight RDLs and other Smith movements but no Smith RDL. Its guided bar path allows a machine-specific hinge setup while retaining the front-of-leg RDL path.
- **Iso-Lateral Wide Chest:** Atlas has horizontal and decline presses, but no upright press that begins with a particularly wide arm position. The manufacturer describes independent converging arms and a wide starting position; this differs from the existing decline machine's conventional start width and path.
- **Ground Base Jammer Press:** Atlas has seated and cable presses, but no standing plate-loaded Jammer with independent converging/diverging lever arms. The athletic split stance, leg drive, and forward/upward path make it a distinct press pattern.
- **Ground Base High Pull:** Atlas has rows and upright rows but no independent-arm, plate-loaded high pull performed from a grounded squat stance. The machine pairs a deep leg drive with a close upward pull; this represents its high-pull function separately from the squat function.
- **Smith Machine Shoulder Press:** Atlas has a selectorized shoulder press and free-weight overhead presses, but no seated Smith press. The Smith uses one guided bar on rails while the selectorized machine uses pivoting press arms.
- **Yates Row:** Atlas's bent-over barbell row is pictured and cued with a pronated grip. Yates described keeping his torso more upright than a conventional near-parallel row; he used underhand for most of his career and later also used overhand. This record documents the underhand version shown in the illustration.
- **Plate-Loaded Reverse Lunge:** Atlas has bodyweight and barbell reverse lunges, but not a guided machine lunge where the front foot stays on a fixed platform while the rear foot moves on an inclined sliding platform. Panatta describes its Super Lunge as an alternating backward lunge with a guided track, separating it from the step-back versions already in Atlas.
- **Overhead Machine Triceps Extension:** Atlas has a seated machine extension with upper arms on a pad and overhead extensions using dumbbells or cables. Panatta's French Press machine keeps the shoulders flexed and upper arms overhead throughout elbow extension; its guided path differs from the pad-supported downward machine extension.
- **Standing Machine Hip Thrust:** Atlas already has a conventional seated machine hip thrust and a single-leg machine glute kickback. Panatta's newer standing unit uses adjustable footrests, a pelvis roller, and a guided bilateral standing hip-extension path, so the torso no longer rests on a bench/backrest. This is a distinct posture and movement setup; it is a newer machine format, not a claim that all gyms have one.
- **Meadows Row:** Atlas has a general Landmine Row, but its record uses a front-facing grip on a handle. John Meadows's named variation stands perpendicular to the bar, grips the loaded sleeve overhand, braces the free hand on the forward thigh, and rows with a wider elbow path. A Strength & Conditioning Journal technique column describes Meadows Row as a separate landmine-row variation with distinct setup and execution.
- **Plate-Loaded Iso-Lateral Row:** Atlas has chest-supported selectorized rows and several plate-loaded rows with distinct paths, but no horizontal row with independent converging/diverging plate-loaded arms and multiple grips. Life Fitness calls this a popular plate-loaded staple; its product page describes the independent arms and chest-supported setup.
- **Plate-Loaded Iso-Lateral Front Lat Pulldown:** Atlas has cable underhand pulldowns and a plate-loaded wide-grip pulldown. The Hammer Strength front pulldown uses independent arms, underhand handles, and a rotating arc down/back/out; it differs from the fixed-bar cable pull and the wide-grip machine path.
- **Smith Machine Decline Bench Press:** Atlas has free-weight and machine decline presses plus Smith flat and incline presses, but no decline bench beneath a guided Smith bar. The bar's guided path combined with the feet-up decline position forms a common chest-press setup distinct from the existing Smith angles and fixed-arm decline machine.
- **Smith Machine Close-Grip Bench Press:** Atlas has a barbell close-grip bench press and a Smith flat press, but not the combined setup. The shoulder-width grip and elbows-near-torso position emphasize elbow extension; ACE documents the grip and execution for the barbell version, while the Smith rails provide the machine-specific guided bar path.
- **Plate-Loaded Seated Dip (existing-record update):** Atlas already had this movement and an older illustration. Its record now cites Hammer Strength's product documentation and clarifies the supported face-in press-down setup; the updated illustration uses the existing record ID. This remains distinct from bodyweight/assisted dips and the forward close-grip machine chest press.
- **Plate-Loaded Iso-Lateral Leg Extension:** Atlas has selectorized bilateral and single-leg leg-extension entries, but not the commercial plate-loaded model with two independent loading arms, a stabilizing belt, and unilateral or bilateral use. The target action is still knee extension, so this is recorded specifically as a machine/equipment variant rather than a new joint movement.
- **Plate-Loaded Iso-Lateral Leg Curl:** Atlas has selectorized prone/seated and kneeling curls, but not the plate-loaded prone machine with independent arms. Its face-down position matches the familiar lying leg curl, while each arm can be trained independently; the manufacturer explicitly distinguishes prone from its kneeling curl by the supported body position.
- **Plate-Loaded Super Fly:** Atlas has a selectorized Pec Deck Fly and free-weight/cable fly paths, but no plate-loaded fly with flat-to-decline angle adjustment plus an adjustable range stop. This is a distinct adjustable machine path, not a new name for the existing fixed Pec Deck.
- **Barbell Reverse Curl:** Atlas has dumbbell and EZ-bar reverse curls, but no straight-bar version. ACE lists the Reverse Bicep Curl as a barbell exercise and describes palms-down grip, elbows kept by the sides, and a controlled curl; the Atlas cues use that common straight-bar setup.
